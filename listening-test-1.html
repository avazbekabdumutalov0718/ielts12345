The professor discusses a case study about a company called Chocozine.

Eric Smith and colleagues were struck by the mass customisation movement that was spreading through a large number of industries in the US.

A European retailer wanted to give chocolate bars as a gift in their high-end stores. Smith knew it could also be very good publicity.

If they failed, there could be damage to their financial situation and their reputation. They only had three machines, which wasn't enough.

Smith couldn't find a subcontractor with experience of the type of melted toppings Chocozine used. Many MBA students thought doing the deal would be a mistake. One student had a contact in the chocolate business, but the new subcontractor had trouble reproducing the distinctive shape of the bars.

Smith declined the order, but sales improved anyway. Taking on too big an order has destroyed many small businesses.
